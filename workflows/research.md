# Stage 2 — Research

Turn the chosen topic into verified facts and tracked sources. Standards are in `references/research-standards.md`; this is the procedure.

## 1. Identify primary sources

Start from the top of the source hierarchy. Ask: what is the *thing itself* here?

- A founder/investor statement → the original interview or transcript.
- A business outcome → the filing, earnings call, or official announcement.
- A market move → the regulator/central-bank/official release.
- A BUILD topic → your own records; external claims still need their own sources.

## 2. Add secondary context

Use reputable secondary sources to confirm interpretation and fill context. Mark them as secondary. Weak sources (unsourced posts, rumors) are leads to chase back, never the basis of a claim.

## 3. Verify

Run the verification rules on every claim you plan to use:

- Every date, statistic, name, number → found in a source? If not, drop it.
- Every quotation → checked against the original? If paraphrased/translated, label it or drop it.
- Freshness → dated? If old, why still relevant?

## 4. Separate fact / interpretation / opinion

As you record, tag each item: **fact** (verifiable), **interpretation** (a reading), **opinion** (a judgment). The post may mix them but must never blur them.

## 5. Record sources

Fill `templates/research-notes.md`: for each claim, its source (name/URL/date) and primary/secondary status. This is the QA trace and the correction trail — do not skip it.

## 6. Stop

Stop when the central claim is verified and the argument has enough support. Do not collect sources for volume. Three solid sources beat fifteen thin ones.

If research turns up something that *breaks* the topic's premise, return to Stage 1 — the topic changed, do not force it.
