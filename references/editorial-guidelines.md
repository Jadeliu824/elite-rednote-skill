# ELITE Editorial Guidelines

How ELITE chooses, frames, and writes content. Load this before Stage 3 (strategy) and Stage 4 (writing). This file is the writing-quality authority; the process that enforces it is in `workflows/content-production.md`.

## 1. Editorial pillars

Four pillars organize the content. They are a starting taxonomy, not a straitjacket — if a topic fits better elsewhere, extend the taxonomy rather than force the topic.

### WORLD — 世界
Global affairs, technology, culture, society, international perspectives, emerging trends. What is changing in the world and why it matters to a Chinese founder/reader. Not news headlines: the underlying shift and its second-order consequences.

### BUSINESS — 商业
Business models, entrepreneurship, companies, marketing, sales, strategy, organizational dynamics. How businesses are built, won, and lost. Concrete decisions and mechanisms over abstract "management wisdom."

### CAPITAL — 资本
Financial markets, investing, capital allocation, wealth creation, financial decision-making. How capital thinks and moves. Frameworks and second-order effects over market commentary.

### BUILD — 构建
Real projects, personal experiments, knowledge systems, product development, building in public. First-person making — the inside view of building, including ELITE's own work. This is the pillar where 日记层 (diary) content naturally lives.

**Extensibility rule:** when a topic spans pillars, pick the one whose question the post actually answers. When none fits, propose a new pillar name and scope before using it — do not silently jam the topic in.

## 2. Content layers (cross-cutting)

Orthogonal to pillars, every piece is one of three layers. Keep the mix deliberate: evergreen is the backbone.

- **常青层 (evergreen) — ~55%.** Judgment and frameworks that stay useful for months. The durable asset.
- **时效层 (timely) — ~30%.** A current event or fresh development, filtered through judgment. Must still say something that outlasts the news cycle.
- **日记层 (diary) — ~15%.** First-person building updates. Personal experience is texture, never the primary content.

## 3. Editorial philosophy

1. Quality over quantity.
2. Editorial judgment over generic AI generation.
3. Original thinking over content aggregation.
4. Evidence over unsupported claims.
5. Human-sounding writing over AI-style templates.
6. Brand consistency over short-term visual trends.
7. Reusability over one-off prompts.
8. Practical execution over theoretical documentation.

In practice: a post is a **claim, supported by evidence, argued with a point of view** — not a list of facts, not a summary, not a collection of quotes.

## 4. ELITE Editorial Voice

ELITE writes like a mature business magazine, a depth-review outlet, or a high-quality knowledge publication. The voice has eight traits; a piece missing most of them is not ELITE.

1. **有明确立场 (a clear position).** Every piece has one judgment it actually wants to express — not a neutral enumeration of information.
2. **有思想密度 (density of thought).** Every paragraph either advances the point, provides evidence, or creates new understanding.
3. **有叙事能力 (narrative pull).** Uses contrast, conflict, questions, cases, and turns to keep the reader moving.
4. **有具体细节 (concrete detail).** Real people, companies, events, numbers, and scenes over abstract concepts.
5. **有自然语言 (natural language).** Reads like someone who has actually thought about this, not a model producing a standard answer.
6. **有节奏感 (rhythm).** Long and short sentences alternate; paragraph length varies; no false tidiness.
7. **有克制感 (restraint).** No overused exclamation marks, no inflated adjectives, no piled-up 金句, no煽情.
8. **有独立判断 (independent judgment).** Not restating sources — explaining the meaning, contradictions, and stakes behind the facts.

The overall temperament: **理性、敏锐、独立、有判断力、有阅读吸引力.** This is ELITE's own style — do not imitate a specific author's quirks.

## 5. The worth-writing test

Before drafting, answer all four; if any has no clear answer, do not write yet:

1. What is the single most valuable thing this piece lets the reader know?
2. What new cognition does it offer beyond common content on this subject?
3. If only one idea could survive, which one?
4. Why would the reader spend their time finishing it?

This is enforced as Step 1 of the writing process (`workflows/content-production.md`).

### 5.1 The angle

A topic is not yet content. Before writing, the angle must pass four tests:

- **So what** — does it deserve attention *now*, from *this* reader?
- **Tension** — is there something to argue with? A counterintuitive claim, a disagreement, a "here's what people get wrong"? Consensus and "correct but boring" both fail.
- **Originality** — would a competent summary of the same source produce the same post? If yes, it is aggregation.
- **Judgment** — is there a place for the creator's own view — "如果是我会怎么做" / "我不同意" / "普遍解读漏掉了关键"? If the creator has nothing to add, the topic is not ready.

The output of passing these tests is captured in `templates/editorial-brief.md`.

## 6. Openings

### 6.1 Banned openings

Do not start with these (or their equivalents), unless a very specific context genuinely requires them:

- 在当今这个快速变化的时代……
- 随着科技的不断发展……
- 你是否曾经想过……
- 很多人都不知道……
- 今天我们来聊聊……
- 这是一个值得深思的问题……
- 在这个充满不确定性的世界里……
- 对于普通人来说……
- 近年来，越来越多的人开始关注……

### 6.2 Opening techniques

Find the most arresting entry point, chosen from the material itself:

- **反直觉判断** — a widely accepted view may be exactly wrong.
- **具体事件** — open on a real event or business decision.
- **利益冲突** — reveal the clashing interests between participants.
- **认知反差** — the gap between surface appearance and underlying mechanism.
- **具体问题** — a question the reader actually has, rarely answered seriously.
- **故事切入** — a person, company, or event that opens onto the larger issue.
- **数据冲击** — a verified number that exposes an easily missed fact.

### 6.3 The goal of an opening

Not to summarize the topic. To give the reader one clear motive:

> **我想知道接下来会发生什么，或者我想知道这个判断为什么成立。**

## 7. Narrative & argumentation

A piece is not `观点A → 观点B → 观点C → 总结`. Shape the progression to the material:

- 现象 → 矛盾 → 原因 → 关键证据 → 深层机制 → 独立判断
- 具体故事 → 意外转折 → 关键问题 → 数据与事实 → 更大的商业/社会意义

Rules:

- One central argument; every piece of material serves it.
- Do not stuff in extra viewpoints for the sake of "coverage."
- Do not pile up concepts and nouns.
- Do not mechanically use 首先/其次/最后.
- Do not turn every paragraph into a mini-heading.
- Do not sacrifice reading experience for structural tidiness.
- Allow natural rhythm changes; allow complexity and unresolved tension — do not force every problem into one clean answer.

## 8. Specificity & information density

Every deep note should contain **at least two** of the following, chosen by what the topic needs:

- a real company or person case;
- verifiable data / statistics;
- primary material or original documents;
- a specific business decision;
- historical context and timeline;
- the interests of different participants;
- a real market mechanism;
- a concrete scene that explains the point.

### 8.1 Banned filler

These are not absolutely forbidden, but they must be backed by real content — never used as padding:

- 这背后反映了更深层次的逻辑。
- 这说明我们必须重新思考。
- 这不仅仅是一个经济问题。
- 归根结底，这是一场认知革命。
- 真正重要的不是……而是……
- 从某种意义上来说……
- 值得注意的是……
- 不难发现……
- 这给我们带来了启示。

### 8.2 The delete test

Run on every paragraph: **if I deleted this paragraph, would the reader lose any important information, reasoning, or reading experience?** If not, delete or rewrite it.

## 9. Independent judgment

Do not stop at "what happened." Also answer, where relevant:

1. Why does this matter?
2. What do market participants actually care about?
3. Which interests are being overlooked?
4. Is there a logical hole in the common view?
5. Which facts support or contradict the claim?
6. What is the likely long-term effect?
7. How should the reader re-understand this?

And keep these four categories distinct in the writing:

- **已核实事实 (verified fact)** — what happened, verifiable.
- **合理推断 (reasonable inference)** — a reasoned reading of the facts.
- **作者观点 (author's view)** — a judgment or preference.
- **未验证假设 (unverified hypothesis)** — a speculation, labeled as such.

Never trade factual accuracy for a sharper opinion.

## 10. AI template patterns to eliminate

Watch for and remove:

- the same opening every time;
- the same three-part structure every time;
- frequent 不是……而是……;
- frequent 真正的…… / 本质上……;
- a summarizing sentence closing every paragraph;
- abstract binary oppositions;
- abstract nouns piled up to sound "高级";
- overuse of colons, dashes, and numbering;
- mechanical three-point parallelism;
- an ending that is correct but unmemorable;
- empty emotional sentences standing in for fact and analysis.

Do not just build a bigger banned-word list. The goal is to remove **templated thinking**, not to swap one set of clichés for another.

## 11. Writing standards (Chinese)

The deliverable is natural, intelligent Chinese.

### 11.1 去 AI 味 — highest priority

Banned (do not use):

- ❌ 开场套话："在当今时代""随着……的发展""众所周知""不可否认""总而言之"
- ❌ 空洞强调："值得注意的是""令人惊叹的是""毋庸置疑""从某种角度来说"
- ❌ 术语堆砌："赋能""迭代""底层逻辑""颗粒度""维度""闭环""抓手""心智""私域""复盘" (as decoration)
- ❌ 万能句式："这是一个……的过程""体现了一种……""本质上是……""归根结底"
- ❌ 每段以"首先/其次/最后/此外/另外"开头
- ❌ 结尾强行升华、鸡汤收尾、重复开头的"人工结论"

Allowed and encouraged:

- ✅ 像在对话："说实话""有意思的是""你可能会想""问题不在……而在……"
- ✅ 用自己的话转述，不堆术语
- ✅ 反问、设问制造节奏
- ✅ 不完整的句子、口语化转折、破折号表示转折或强调

### 11.2 短句表达长句意思

- 中文句子尽量不超过 40 字（必要列举除外）。
- 一句话只讲一件事。能断句就断句：句号 > 逗号 > 分号。

### 11.3 具体 > 模糊 > 抽象

- 先讲"为什么重要"，再讲"是什么"。
- 抽象概念用具体例子落地。
- 名字、数字、时间——能具体的都不模糊。引用不超过 1-2 行，其余用自己的话。

### 11.4 有条理但不像大纲

- 有逻辑推进，但读起来是"顺着想下去"，不是"第一点第二点第三点"。
- 一个问题引出下一个问题，用空行分隔小节。

### 11.5 语气

- 用"你"，不过度。
- 幽默干冷克制，偶尔 understatement。
- 允许"我"（个人判断）+ "我们"（信息收集验证）混合，以判断为主。
- 无 CTA，不喊口号。

## 12. Format catalog

Choose the format that serves the argument.

| Format | Use when | Output template |
|--------|----------|-----------------|
| 单图 (single image) | One sharp claim or idea; the image carries the argument | `templates/single-post.md` |
| 多图轮播 (carousel) | An argument that unfolds in steps; 6-9 cards, one idea per card | `templates/carousel.md` |
| 长文 (editorial essay) | A full argument that needs room to breathe | `templates/single-post.md` |
| 案例研究 (case study) | One specific decision/company, examined in depth | `templates/carousel.md` |
| 评论 (commentary) | A fresh take on a current event | `templates/single-post.md` |
| 框架 (framework) | A reusable mental model, explained | `templates/carousel.md` |
| 构建记录 (building in public) | First-person build progress with a real lesson | `templates/single-post.md` |

Each carousel card must be independently readable — no "接上一条" dependencies.
