# ELITE Platform Guidelines (Xiaohongshu)

Load this before Stage 6 (optimization) and Stage 8 (performance review). Covers how to optimize for Xiaohongshu search and how to read results — without sacrificing editorial quality.

## 1. Compliance first

ELITE follows platform rules strictly and never risks the account for reach. Standing rules:

- No 硬广, no CTA (no 私信/评论/关注 solicitations), no fake engagement.
- No fabricated claims presented as fact (see `research-standards.md`).
- No impersonation, no misleading editing.
- Titles and covers describe the actual content — no 标题党 bait that the post does not deliver.
- When a topic touches a sensitive area (political, financial advice, health), stay on the safe, factual side or drop it. ELITE publishes judgment, not regulatory exposure.

Compliance is a hard filter. If reach and compliance conflict, compliance wins.

## 2. What Xiaohongshu actually rewards

Understand the mechanism before optimizing:

- **Cover + title** drive click-through from the feed. A post is not read if it is not opened.
- **First line** of the description/body decides whether a scroller keeps reading.
- **Search** is a real acquisition channel: users search specific keywords, and posts with matching title + text + hashtags surface over time (a post can be discovered weeks later).
- **Saves** signal durable value far more than likes; saves are the strongest engagement signal for ELITE's evergreen layer.

## 3. Search optimization (Stage 6)

Goal: be found by people searching for the *question* the post answers — without keyword stuffing.

1. **Identify search keywords** the reader would actually type (Chinese, natural phrasing). Ask: what would someone search to reach this insight? Prefer the concrete over the abstract ("判断力" is abstract; "创始人 决策 案例" is searchable).
2. **Title first.** Write the title for a human, then ensure the key searchable term appears naturally. Never force a keyword if it makes the title worse.
3. **Description.** The description repeats the searchable terms once, in prose — not as a list of tags.
4. **Hashtags.** A small, relevant set (3-8), mixing a broad category tag and specific topic tags. No tag-stuffing, no unrelated trending tags.

The rule: **search is a tailwind, not the editor.** Editorial quality is never sacrificed to the algorithm. If a keyword fight is lost, the readable title wins.

## 4. Publishing copy

The `publishing-copy` template (Stage 6 output) captures: final title, search keywords, hashtags, and the description. The description's first line is the most important sentence after the cover — make it earn the scroll.

## 5. Performance metrics (Stage 8)

Treat metrics as three separate layers; do not collapse them into one "good/bad" number.

| Layer | Metrics | What it tells you |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| **Reach** | impressions, views, CTR (when available) | Did the cover + title get the post opened? Low reach → cover/title problem. |
| **Engagement** | likes, saves, comments, shares | Did the content land? Saves >> likes for evergreen value. High views + low saves → the post was opened but not kept. |
| **Business outcome** | profile visits, leads, conversions | Did it move anyone toward the goal? For ELITE this is currently soft (no hard CTA), so treat as directional. |

**Interpreting results — the learning loop:**

- Reach low, engagement normal → fix cover/title in the next cycle.
- Reach high, saves low → the argument did not deliver durable value; sharpen the angle, not the packaging.
- Saves high, comments low → strong utility; comments are a lagging or discussion-gated signal, not a failure.
- A low-traffic post with high saves can still be a *better* post than a high-traffic post with low saves. Do not chase traffic blindly.

Record learnings in the `performance-review` workflow and feed them into the next Stage 1 (`workflows/performance-review.md` → `workflows/topic-discovery.md`).
